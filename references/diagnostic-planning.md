# Adaptive Diagnostic and Planning

## Principle

Diagnosis is an interaction embedded throughout learning, not a compulsory quiz at the end. Sample the smallest output that can change the next teaching decision.

## Diagnostic Loop

1. Choose the highest-information unresolved node.
2. Ask for one short output in the form the target capability requires.
3. Classify the evidence as `unseen`, `developing`, `secure`, or `transfer-ready`.
4. Record the specific strength, misconception, or missing prerequisite.
5. Advance, support narrowly, step back, or test a nearby variant.

Do not pre-send later questions whose content depends on the current answer.

## Evidence Options

- explain a relationship in the learner's own words
- distinguish two nearby concepts using a scenario
- predict before calculating, running code, or experimenting
- complete or modify one worked step
- interpret a result, shape, unit, plot, log, or observation
- choose and justify a method under constraints
- find and correct an error
- transfer the idea to a changed case

Use a batch quiz and numerical score only when the learner explicitly requests a test, exam simulation, or portable assessment artifact. Avoid generic fixed score bands when node-level evidence gives a more useful decision.

## Planning From Evidence

- Preserve dependency order and cut breadth first.
- Allocate time to unresolved core nodes, not evenly across topics.
- Define checkpoints by learner outputs rather than elapsed days.
- Put optional branches after the target capability is secure.
- Re-plan when evidence changes; do not defend the original schedule.

## Progress Summary

At a natural checkpoint, report only:

- target capability
- evidence already shown
- current unresolved node
- next action and why

Do not mistake resource completion, chat length, or an AI-generated answer for progress.
